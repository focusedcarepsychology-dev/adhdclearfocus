ADHD and autism overlap: similar-looking traits can have different mechanisms

ADHD and autism are distinct neurodevelopmental conditions that can occur together. Current adult research confirms substantial trait overlap, but overlap does not make the conditions interchangeable.

Short answer: A person can meet criteria for ADHD, autism, both or neither. Attention, sensory regulation, routines, social communication, executive function and intense interests can interact in ways that require a broader developmental assessment rather than one questionnaire.

Where confusion can happen

Attention and interests

Intense engagement can occur in both conditions, while the reasons and patterns may differ.

Social difficulty

Impulsive interruption, missed cues, social-communication differences and overwhelm can produce similar outcomes through different pathways.

Routines

One person may crave routine yet struggle to maintain it because executive-function difficulties interfere.

Sensory load

Sensory sensitivities can alter attention, regulation and social capacity and deserve direct exploration.

Why combined assessment can matter

If both patterns are plausible, assessing only one condition can produce an incomplete formulation. Developmental history, context, functional impact and differential explanations remain central.

Source

Waldren et al. (2024), multi-method research on autism–ADHD overlap in adults.
