ADHD procrastination: delay is not always a motivation problem

A 2026 systematic review found a consistent positive association between ADHD symptoms and procrastination, with inattention the most robust correlate. Most studies are cross-sectional, so the evidence supports association rather than a simple one-way cause.

Short answer: ADHD-related procrastination can involve difficulty initiating, weak time salience, uncertainty about the first step, emotional avoidance, low immediate reward and executive overload. “Care more” is therefore often a poor intervention.

Why urgency can temporarily solve it

A close deadline creates immediate salience, narrows options and supplies external structure. That can make a previously vague task suddenly actionable — but relying on crisis-level urgency repeatedly is exhausting and risky.

Replace the deadline spike with smaller signals

Define a physical first step

“Open the spreadsheet and enter the first row” is easier to initiate than “sort finances”.

Use artificial starts

Set a ten-minute start window rather than requiring completion.

Bring consequences closer

Use interim milestones, visible progress and short check-ins rather than one distant final deadline.

Address avoidance

If shame, fear or uncertainty drives delay, reducing the emotional threat may matter as much as better scheduling.

Source

Suriano (2026), systematic review of ADHD and procrastination.
