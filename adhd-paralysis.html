ADHD paralysis: when knowing what to do is not enough to start

“ADHD paralysis” is not a formal diagnosis. It is a useful everyday description for the experience of feeling stuck despite intention, urgency or consequences.

Short answer: What people call ADHD paralysis often reflects task-initiation difficulty, competing priorities, working-memory load, emotional avoidance, low immediate reward or uncertainty about the first step.

Why starting can be harder than doing

ADHD involves difficulties in executive functions that help organise behaviour toward a goal. A task can be objectively simple but still demand several hidden operations: choosing where to begin, holding steps in mind, estimating time, tolerating boredom and switching away from something more rewarding.

Strategies that reduce the activation cost

Shrink the first action

Replace “finish the report” with an action that takes under two minutes: open the document, write the heading, or list three bullets.

Externalise the sequence

Put the next three steps somewhere visible. This reduces the need to hold the whole task in working memory.

Use a short timer

Commit to ten minutes rather than completion. The goal is to cross the starting threshold.

Add another person

Body doubling — working alongside another person — can provide structure and accountability even when they are doing a different task.

When “paralysis” may be something else

Severe fatigue, depression, anxiety, sleep deprivation, medication effects and physical illness can all create similar difficulty initiating tasks. Persistent or worsening changes deserve professional assessment rather than being automatically attributed to ADHD.
