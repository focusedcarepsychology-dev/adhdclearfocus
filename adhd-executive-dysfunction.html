ADHD executive dysfunction: when the plan exists but the action stalls

Executive function is a useful way to describe goal-management processes such as planning, inhibition, working memory and switching. ADHD is associated with executive-function difficulties, but they are neither universal nor unique to ADHD.

Short answer: Adults with ADHD, as a group, show measurable executive-function difficulties, but neuropsychological performance varies widely. In everyday life, the most useful response is often to externalise the executive work rather than expecting working memory and self-initiation to carry everything internally.

Common everyday demands

Initiation

Starting a task without waiting for panic, novelty or external pressure.

Working memory

Holding instructions and next steps in mind while doing something else.

Switching

Stopping one activity and moving deliberately to another.

Inhibition

Creating a pause before acting on a competing impulse or distraction.

Externalise the function

Visible task lists, written priorities, timers, checklists, templates, calendar blocks and environmental cues reduce how much executive work has to happen invisibly in the moment.

Sources

Boonstra et al. (2005), meta-analytic review of executive functioning in adult ADHD · NICE NG87.
