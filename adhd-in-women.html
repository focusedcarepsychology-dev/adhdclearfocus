ADHD in women: why recognition can come later

Girls and women can have the same disorder while showing a pattern that attracts less external attention — particularly when hyperactivity is less obvious or difficulties are heavily masked.

Short answer: ADHD can be missed when symptoms are quieter, compensated for, interpreted as anxiety or perfectionism, or become obvious only when adult demands exceed coping strategies.

Why childhood ADHD can be overlooked

A child who daydreams, loses belongings, works slowly, forgets instructions or compensates through high effort may attract less concern than a child whose behaviour disrupts the classroom. Diagnostic systems do not define a separate “female ADHD”, but presentation and social expectations can affect recognition.

Masking and compensation

Some adults build elaborate systems to avoid mistakes: over-preparing, checking repeatedly, arriving very early, working late or relying on anxiety to create urgency. These strategies can conceal impairment while increasing exhaustion.

Why demands matter

Difficulties can become more visible during university, parenthood, higher-responsibility work, hormonal transitions or other periods when the amount of planning and self-management increases.

What an assessment should consider

A careful assessment looks beyond stereotypes. It examines developmental history, functioning across settings, impairment, co-occurring conditions and alternative explanations. Anxiety and ADHD can coexist; one should not automatically exclude the other.

Sources

Faraone SV et al. (2021), International Consensus Statement; Quinn PO & Madhoo M (2014), review of ADHD in women and girls; NICE NG87.
