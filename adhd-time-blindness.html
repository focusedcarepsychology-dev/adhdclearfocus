ADHD time blindness: making invisible time visible

Time blindness is an informal term for difficulty sensing, estimating and acting on time. It is not a standalone diagnostic criterion, but time-management problems are common in ADHD.

Short answer: ADHD can make it harder to estimate how long something will take, feel the approach of a deadline, stop a rewarding activity or begin a task whose payoff is distant.

What it can look like

Common experiences include repeatedly underestimating travel time, losing hours in hyperfocus, starting only when a deadline becomes urgent, forgetting future intentions and feeling that there are only two times: “now” and “not now”.

Make time external

Use visible countdowns

A timer turns an abstract future limit into a changing signal you can see and hear.

Plan backwards

Start with the time you must arrive or finish, then work backwards to include transition and preparation time.

Add transition alarms

Use one alert to prepare to switch and a second to actually switch.

Track real durations

For a week, record how long repeat tasks actually take. Replace estimates with your own data.

Time difficulties are not unique to ADHD

Sleep deprivation, anxiety, depression, autism, stress and demanding environments can also alter attention and time management. Context matters.

Sources
