ADHD at work: performance problems are often system problems too

ADHD can affect occupational functioning, but workplace design matters. Current research points to both genuine work impairment and a shortage of strong context-specific intervention studies — so support should be practical, individualised and reviewed.

Short answer: ADHD can affect organisation, time management, sustained attention, interpersonal functioning and safety in some roles. Recent reviews also show that evidence for specific workplace interventions is still limited, making individual problem-solving more useful than universal “ADHD hacks”.

Where work can create unnecessary friction

Constant interruption

Open-plan noise and notification-heavy workflows can repeatedly reset attention.

Invisible priorities

When every request looks equally urgent, executive load increases rapidly.

Long unstructured projects

Distant deadlines provide little immediate signal for task initiation or sequencing.

Safety-critical tasks

Roles involving vehicles, machinery or other hazards require particular attention to fatigue, distraction and safe systems of work.

Practical supports

Written priorities, protected focus periods, interim milestones, predictable check-ins, quieter work options where feasible and clear task ownership are examples of changes that can reduce friction without lowering essential standards.
