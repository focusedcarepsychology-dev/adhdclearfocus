ADHD workplace adjustments: change the system, not the person

Many helpful ADHD accommodations are inexpensive process changes: make priorities visible, reduce unnecessary switching and build clearer external structure.

Short answer: Useful adjustments depend on the individual and the role, but common themes include clearer written priorities, quieter working options, structured check-ins, reduced task switching and flexibility in how focused work is organised.

Examples of practical adjustments

Written priorities

Confirm the top one to three priorities in writing, especially after meetings or when priorities change.

Protected focus time

Reduce avoidable meetings and notifications during work that requires sustained concentration.

Clear deadlines and milestones

Break large projects into visible interim dates rather than relying on one distant final deadline.

Predictable check-ins

Short regular reviews can prevent uncertainty from accumulating and make course correction easier.

Individualise rather than stereotype

Two employees with ADHD may need very different support. Adjustments should be based on actual functional barriers, the essential requirements of the role and collaborative discussion.
