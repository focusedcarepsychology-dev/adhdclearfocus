ADHD body doubling: borrowed structure for difficult starts

Body doubling means doing your own task while another person is present — physically or remotely. Many people with ADHD report that the shared structure makes starting and staying with a task easier.

Short answer: Body doubling is a practical behavioural strategy rather than a recognised medical treatment. Direct research on the term itself is still limited, so it should be described as a low-risk strategy people may find useful rather than a proven ADHD intervention.

Why it may help

Another person can make time and intention more visible, reduce opportunities to drift into unrelated tasks, and create a mild social commitment to remain engaged. The other person does not need to supervise you or work on the same task.

How to try it

Define the first task

Say what you plan to do during the session in one sentence.

Use a time boundary

Choose a short interval and agree when you will check in again.

Keep interaction light

The goal is co-presence, not a conversation that replaces the work.

Protect privacy

Do not share confidential work, client information or sensitive documents with an online group.

Evidence caveat

There is good broader evidence for external structure and behavioural strategies in ADHD, but body doubling as a named intervention has a smaller direct evidence base. ADHDclearfocus therefore presents it as a practical option rather than a clinically established treatment.
