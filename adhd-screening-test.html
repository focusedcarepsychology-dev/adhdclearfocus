ADHD screening tests: useful gatekeepers, not diagnoses

A good ADHD screener can identify whether your pattern is worth exploring further. It cannot establish a diagnosis on its own.

Short answer: ADHD screeners estimate whether symptoms and life-impact patterns are consistent with ADHD. A diagnosis requires a broader clinical assessment, including developmental history, impairment, differential diagnosis and context.

What an ADHD screener is designed to do

Screening tools are designed to be sensitive enough to flag people who may benefit from further assessment. Widely used adult measures include the WHO Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS). A positive screen means “look more closely”, not “you definitely have ADHD”.

Why screening and diagnosis are different

ADHD symptoms overlap with sleep problems, anxiety, depression, trauma, autism, substance use, physical health conditions and periods of extreme stress. A proper diagnostic process considers alternative explanations and whether difficulties were present across development and settings.

What to bring to a clinical assessment

Developmental history

Examples from childhood, school reports where available, and information from someone who knew you when you were younger can be helpful.

Current impact

Concrete examples from work, study, relationships, organisation, driving, finances, sleep and daily responsibilities are more useful than labels alone.
